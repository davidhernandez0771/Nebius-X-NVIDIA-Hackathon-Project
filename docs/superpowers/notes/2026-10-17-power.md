# Power note: how many eval episodes per arm (sprint 7, T1)

**For T3.** Every number below comes from `demo_pipeline/stats.py` (tested against published values in
`tests/test_stats.py`). The analysis test is **two-sided Fisher exact, α = 0.05**, as for the sprint 6 result.

## Recommendation

- **Target: n = 70 per arm** (seeds 5000–5069; 5000–5019 are already done, so add **5020–5069**).
  - That gives about **80% power to tell 10% from 30%**.
  - It costs about **2.1 h** for both arms at ~77 s per episode.
- **Minimum worth running: n = 40 per arm** (add 5020–5039, about 0.9 h).
  - It detects **10% vs 40%** with 85% power.
  - It has only about a coin flip's chance (53%) of detecting 10% vs 30%.
- **Don't try to detect 10% vs 20%.** It needs about **215 per arm** (about 9 h of eval at the current speed).
  That is out of reach until T2's parallel eval lands.

**How to run it:**
1. **Alternate the arms in blocks of 10 seeds** (raw 5020–5029, mult 5020–5029, raw 5030–5039, …). If
   time runs out, both arms then have the same n.
2. **Fix the stopping point by time or by n, never by the result.** Do not stop early because p dropped
   below 0.05 (or keep going because it didn't). Peeking at p while deciding whether to continue
   inflates the false-positive rate.
3. **Report the n you reached, the counts, the Wilson CIs and the Fisher p,** whatever they say.
4. **Pick the "best pair" for the extension before you see the extended results.** Use the 20-scene
   results (or the 30k checkpoints, if that is what step 2 produced), and write down which pair it is
   and why, **first**.

## Episodes per arm to detect 10% vs p2 (α = 0.05 two-sided, power 0.8)

| p2 | Fleiss, normal approx. | Fleiss, continuity-corrected | exact Fisher (smallest n with power ≥ 0.8) | eval time, both arms, from 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20% | 199 | 219 | **214** | ~9.2 h |
| 30% | 62 | 72 | **69** | ~3.0 h |
| 40% | 32 | 38 | **36** | ~1.5 h |

- The exact-Fisher column comes from `fisher_power`, which enumerates every outcome.
- The uncorrected formula is too optimistic for Fisher's test: at n = 199, its power for 10% vs 20% is
  0.77, not 0.80. **Use the corrected or the exact column.**

## What 2/20 vs 3/20 can and cannot say

| cell | successes | rate | Wilson 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| `raw_10` | 2/20 | 10% | **2.8% – 30.1%** |
| `raw_multiply_10` | 3/20 | 15% | **5.2% – 36.0%** |

The Fisher exact two-sided p is **1.0**.

**It can say:**
- Neither policy is good yet. Both upper bounds are near 30–36%, so neither is likely to succeed even
  half the time on unseen home-start scenes.
- Multiplying did not make things **dramatically** worse or better at 10k steps. A jump to ≥ 50% would
  very likely have shown up.

**It cannot say:**
- **Whether multiplying helps.** The intervals overlap almost entirely.
- At n = 20 the test had only **24% power** for a true 10% → 30% improvement, and **7%** for 10% → 20%.
  So "no significant difference" is **not** evidence of "no effect". It is mostly evidence that n = 20
  is too small.
- Even 2/20 vs 9/20 is needed before Fisher calls a difference significant (p = 0.031). At n = 20, only
  an effect of about **10% → 53%** is detected reliably (80% power).
- **Whether the +1 success is real.** If the true rates were exactly 10% and 15%, Fisher would detect
  that with 3% probability at n = 20. Telling 10% from 15% needs about **726 per arm**.

**Wording for the README and video:**
- Good: "2/20 vs 3/20 (Wilson 95% CIs 3–30% and 5–36%; Fisher p = 1.0): no detectable difference at
  this sample size."
- Not allowed: "multiplying improved success by 50%".
- Not allowed: "multiplying doesn't help".
