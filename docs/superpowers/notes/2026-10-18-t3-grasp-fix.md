# T3 grasp fix (2026-10-18): why the multiplied policy drops the cube, and a Neaten-step fix

Branch `pm/grasp-fix` from `origin/perfect-then-multiply` (`e94d817`).

**Result: 20/40 vs 17/40, two-sided Fisher p = 0.65. No detectable difference; not an improvement by the
pre-registered rule (≥ 27/40).**
- **Diagnosis:** the drops start at the approach. The policy arrives above the cube ~2–3 cm off and ~25° turned and
  never corrects, so a finger lands on the cube top or the fingers pinch its edges. The grip and the lift are fine.
- **Fix:** a Neaten-step centered, aligned approach in every demo. All 110 demos verified.
- **Effect:** the policy learned to hover and fix its yaw (median 5° off at the close), but hardly its position.
  Drops fell 22 → 14 and 3 release collisions appeared.
- The details and next steps are at the end.

**Goal:** raise the trained policy above **17/40**. That is `raw_multiply_10_30k` (10×10), on seeds 5020–5059,
serial eval (sprint 7). Its failures are almost all drops: 22 of 23.

**Rules for this track:**
- Fix it in the Neaten step (repair/refine), never by making the physics easier.
- Every copy must still pass the unchanged physics check.
- Pre-register the test before training.
- Use the GPU only under `C:\dev\gpu.lock` with KeepAwake held.

## Step 1: diagnosis

### 1a. What the existing rollouts can and can't say (no GPU)

**The sprint 7/8 evals kept only per-episode summaries.** Each `.episodes.jsonl` line has the reasons, the
ended_by, the step count and the final cube position. There are no per-step signals: no finger targets, contact
times or TCP path. So the existing rollouts can't directly answer "grasp, lift, carry or release?", "fully
closed?" or "opened early?". What they can say:

**Where the cube ended up.** I rebuilt every seed's scene (`sim_eval.scenes.eval_scenes`) and placed each failed
episode's final cube position relative to the cube's start and the bin. Seeds 5020–5059, confirmatory set:

| Cell | Failures | Median distance from the cube's start | Within 5 cm of the start | Along the pick→bin line (0 = start, 1 = bin) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10×10 (`raw_multiply_10_30k`) | 23 | **1.1 cm** | 22 / 23 | 22 at < 0.25; none in 0.25–0.75; 1 at the bin |
| 10×30 (`raw_multiply_10x30`) | 24 | **2.0 cm** | 21 / 24 (the rest at 4.8–6.6 cm) | 23 at < 0.25; none in 0.25–0.75; 1 at the bin |

- Every dropped cube ends upright on the table (z = 0.025) at the spot it was picked from. None lies along the
  carry path, which is 31–48 cm long, and none lies next to the bin.
- So the cube is lost **at the grasp or during the lift, before any horizontal carry.**
  - It is not lost during the carry or at the release.
  - `dropped` itself means contact was lost while the gripper was commanded closed, outside the bin's footprint.
    So the fingers had the cube and lost it.
- **Every drop also ends with `gripper_closed`.** After losing the cube the policy never reopens to try again. It
  holds a closed, empty gripper until the 30 s limit.
- The two non-drop failures are 10×10 5049 (`not_settled`, cube on the bin rim at z 0.11) and 10×30 5034 (in the
  bin, gripper still closed).

**No scene feature predicts a drop.** I pooled the two multiplied cells (100 episodes: 10×10 on 5000–5059 and
10×30 on 5020–5059) and split each feature at its median:

| Feature | Success, low half | Success, high half | Fisher p |
|---|---|---|---|
| cube yaw mod 90° (\|·\|) | 21/50 | 19/50 | 0.84 |
| cube x | 23/51 | 17/49 | 0.31 |
| cube y | 24/50 | 16/50 | 0.15 |
| cube distance from home | 22/50 | 18/50 | 0.54 |
| pick→bin distance | 21/50 | 19/50 | 0.84 |
| bin x / bin y | 21/51 / 16/50 | 19/49 / 24/50 | 0.84 / 0.15 |

- **Cube yaw was my first suspect.** The expert turns the wrist to the cube's yaw mod 90°, and that target flips
  sign at ±45°. But the 30–45° bin succeeds 13/26, no worse than 0–15° (17/40).
- Together with the sprint 8 finding (the two multiplied policies share only 7 of their successes), the drop looks
  like a **low-margin grasp that fails stochastically**, not a set of hard scenes.

**The training demos teach a clean grasp.** All 110 episodes of the 10×10 cell (scratch `grasp_stats.py`,
`train_anatomy.py`):

| Quantity | min / median / max |
|---|---|
| TCP-to-cube offset at the close, xy | 2.3 / 2.7 / 3.6 mm |
| TCP-to-cube offset at the close, z | −3.2 / −1.8 / −1.0 mm |
| finger target while closed (`action[7:9]`) | 0.000 (always fully closed) |
| finger position while holding | 0.025 m = the cube's half-width (one episode 0.035: a diagonal grasp) |
| contact flicker during the hold | 0 steps |
| cube slip in the fingers during the carry | 0.004 / 0.034 / 0.069 mm |
| lift starts (TCP +5 mm) after the close | 0.70 / 0.71 / 0.72 s |
| peak lift speed / acceleration | 0.29 m/s / 0.91 m/s² |

So the policy is not copying bad grasps: every demo closes centered, fully, and waits 0.7 s before lifting. The
drop comes from how the policy *executes* the grasp. Per-step data from its own rollouts is needed to say which part.

### 1b. Traced diagnostic rollouts (GPU)

To get that data I added per-step traces to the eval: `sim_eval.recording.StepTracer`, with `run_eval --trace`
and `eval_cells --trace` (commit `c181547`, tests first). Each trace records, every control step:
- the observed joints, including the finger positions;
- the policy's 9-joint action, including the finger targets;
- the TCP pose, the cube's position and orientation, and the contacts.

The diagnostic run uses the 10×10 30k policy on **20 fresh seeds, 5200–5219**. That is the same checkpoint, start
state, start pose and 30 s limit as the headline eval. **The test seeds 5020–5059 are deliberately not used for
the diagnosis**, so the fix is not tuned on them.

**Run:** 13:06–13:26 under the GPU lock (`T3 41548 grasp-diag-eval-10x10`, released at exit) with KeepAwake. Serial
`eval_cells --checkpoint raw_multiply_10_30k=… --seed0 5200 --n 20 --start-state …sprint6_home_a…\datasets\raw_10
--start-pose dataset --trace`. 0 retries, 0 sim/policy errors.

**Result: 8 / 20** (40%, in line with the headline 17/40 = 42.5%). Failures: 11 `dropped` and 1 `collision` (5215:
the cube ended in the bin but the arm pressed on something with > 5 N).

**When does the cube slip?** In every drop it is lost **at the grasp or in the first few cm of the lift**:
- 7 of 11 drops never lifted the cube at all (cube max z ≤ 0.034 m, against 0.25 m in a good carry).
- The other 4 lifted it 1.4–1.7 cm before it slid out.
- None was lost in the carry or at the release. This matches the 40 headline failures in 1a.

**Is the gripper not fully closed? No.**
- The commanded finger target is fully closed (−0.001 to 0.001 m median) in every grasp.
- The fingers close all the way to 0.000–0.002 m, i.e. on nothing. They stop at the cube's 0.025 m only in the
  successes and in 5215.

**Is the lift too fast? No.** Peak lift speed is 0.19–0.35 m/s, against 0.29 in the demos. The lift starts
0.86–1.84 s after the close, *later* than the demos' 0.71 s.

**Is the policy opening early? No.** No drop has an opening before the loss. Every drop ends `gripper_closed`: the
policy holds a closed, empty gripper to the end and never retries.

**Is the grasp off-center? Yes. This is the cause.** The policy's hand arrives above the cube misaligned, in both
position and wrist yaw, and never corrects on the way down (scratch `approach_axes.py`, `trace_drops.py`).

TCP-to-cube offset in the hand frame (y = the fingers' closing axis), at the first finger contact or the close:

| | n | closing-axis offset \|y\|, median | \|y\| > 15 mm | TCP height at the close (cube center 0.025) | wrist-yaw error vs the cube's faces, median (range) | yaw error ≥ 35° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| successes | 8 | 8.6 mm | 0 / 8 | 0.033 m | 11° (3–28°) | 0 / 8 |
| failures | 12 | 17.4 mm | 7 / 12 | 0.056 m | 32° (9–44°) | 6 / 12 |

There are two ways the misaligned hand loses the cube:
1. **Finger lands on the cube top (7 drops: 5200–5202, 5205, 5206, 5209, 5210).**
   - The hand is 18–31 mm off along the closing axis. The open fingers are 2 × 40 mm around a 50 mm cube, so
     that axis has only ±15 mm of clearance.
   - One fingertip touches the cube's top face with the gripper still open, and the descent stops at TCP z ≈ 0.058,
     3 cm above the cube's center.
   - The gripper then closes on the top edge or on air. The cube tips (z rises to 0.031) and slides out as the
     lift starts.
2. **Diagonal, off-center pinch (4 drops: 5211, 5213, 5216, 5217).**
   - The hand reaches the right height (TCP z 0.034–0.037), but 16–37 mm off-center and turned 26–44° from the
     cube's faces.
   - The fingers close on the cube's edges and squeeze it out sideways: the fingers end at 0.000–0.002 m.
   - In 5211 and 5213 the cube was lifted 1.4–1.7 cm and slid 16–25 mm down the fingers before it fell.

**Both errors are already there at the top of the descent and never shrink.**
- The lateral offset is median 21 mm at z = 0.15 and 26 mm at the contact.
- The yaw error at z = 0.15 is within 10° of the yaw error at the close in all 20 episodes (within 6° in 16).
- In 3 drops (5205, 5209, 5210) the lateral offset even *grows* during the descent, from 6–9 mm to 26–38 mm: the
  hand drifts sideways while going down.

**Successes have errors too, but smaller ones.**
- 3 of the 8 successes also touched the cube top first (5212, 5214, 5218). Their closing-axis offsets were only
  11–14 mm, so the finger slid off the edge and the hand still reached the cube's center.
- The yaw error does not cluster at cubes near ±45°, where the expert's wrist target flips sign. It occurs at every
  cube yaw (e.g. 5206: cube −9°, wrist error +30°).

**Why the policy never corrects:** the demos never show a correction.
- All 110 training episodes arrive above the cube centered to 3 mm and already at the right yaw, then descend
  straight down.
- So the policy has never seen "the cube is off-center in the wrist camera, so move toward it". It carries its
  ~2 cm / ~25° aiming error straight into the grasp.
- ACT also runs each 100-step chunk (2 s) open loop. A correction can only come from a chunk that starts while the
  hand is above the cube.

## Step 2: the fix, in the Neaten step — a centered, aligned approach

**What changes in every demo** (`sim_worker.homeset.rehome(..., approach_offset, approach_yaw)`, driven by
`home_dataset.py --centered-approach`):
1. **Arrive misaligned.** The first move (home → above the cube) arrives at the above-cube pose shifted
   horizontally by an offset and turned about the vertical by a yaw. Both come from `approach_misalignment(seed)`:
   - the offset is uniform over a disc of radius 3 cm (the policy's measured error was ~2 cm);
   - the yaw is uniform in ±35° (the policy's measured error was 3–44°; past 45° the nearer cube face flips);
   - each episode seed has its own stream, independent of its scene.
2. **Hover** there for 0.2 s with the gripper open.
3. **Center and align** in one slow min-jerk move (0.05 m/s average, at least 0.6 s): a horizontal translation at
   the hover height plus the wrist turn back to the grasp yaw.

**What does not change:**
- From the above-cube pose on, every demo is identical to the variant it was (tested): the same vertical descent, grasp
  (full close, 0.5 s hold), lift, carry, release and retreat.
- The physics, the scene, the cube and the success check are untouched.
- Every source and copy is still replayed in Isaac with cameras and verified by the same `check_success`. Only
  verified episodes enter training.

**Why this is the fix the evidence asks for:**
- The policy's grip, lift and release are already as good as the demos'. Its failure is aiming.
- The demos now show, ~110 times, the one thing the policy never saw: the cube off-center and turned in the wrist
  camera, followed by the hand moving over it and turning to its faces before descending.
- **Timing check against ACT's 2 s open-loop chunks.** In 108 of the 110 planned demos, the second chunk's start
  (t = 2.0 s) falls inside the hover or centering window. That chunk is predicted while the wrist camera looks down
  at a misaligned cube, which is exactly where a correction can be learned.

**What it does not address:**
- 5215's `collision` (1 of 12 diagnostic failures): the arm pressed on something after placing the cube.
- The policy never retrying after a drop.

### What the traces say (descriptive, not tested)

- **The policy learned the new behaviour.** In every episode it stops above the cube for ~1.8 s (median) at the
  hover height before descending. It never did this before.
- **It learned the yaw correction.** The wrist-yaw error falls from 20–24° when it enters the hover to 8° when it
  leaves (medians). At the close, the median yaw error is 5° in both successes and failures, against 11° / 32° in
  the diagnostic run. Only 4 of 40 grasps are ≥ 35° off, against 6 of 20 before.
- **It barely learned the position correction.**
  - The lateral offset falls from 17 to 15 mm in successes and from 33 to 32 mm in failures.
  - Only 10 of 40 episodes shrink it by more than 30%: 8 successes and 2 failures.
  - The failures enter the hover a median **33 mm** off. That is more than any offset the new demos show a
    correction from (at most 30 mm), so they are outside what was taught.
- **So the remaining drops are the first mode from step 1, "finger lands on the cube top".**
  - Failures: 12/20 have a closing-axis offset > 15 mm and close at TCP z 0.057, on top of the cube.
  - Successes: 1/20 has an offset > 15 mm, and they close at z 0.031.
- **Drops fell from 22 to 14,** while 3 new `collision` failures appeared (5026, 5030, 5039).
  - In all three, the cube is already in the bin and the gripper open. Then the hand, at the release height (TCP z
    ≈ 0.10), presses on the bin wall with 8–139 N.
  - Under the rules they count as failures. Without them the count would be 23/40, still short of 27, and that is
    not how the test was defined.
  - 3 further failures (5028, 5051, 5054) end with an empty, closed gripper and no `dropped`: the fingers closed
    without ever getting the cube.

### Reading, honestly

- **The diagnosis held up.** The aiming errors were the cause, and the Neaten data changed the policy's aiming:
  yaw almost fully, position hardly at all.
- **The fix did not move the success rate by a detectable amount.**
  - The 3-point gain (and 22 → 14 drops) could be noise. One training run per cell; each seed is close to a coin
    flip (sprint 8).
  - The new collisions at the release ate some of the gain.
- **Caveats:**
  - one training run;
  - a single task;
  - the baseline arms are reused from sprints 7/8, not re-run;
  - equal steps, not equal epochs (≈ 4.2 vs 4.5);
  - the baseline resumed 10k → 30k, while this run went straight to 30k.

**What I would try next.** These are not run: the pre-registration rules out a second training run chosen on this
result.
1. Wider position offsets in the demos, ≥ 5 cm. The failing policies arrive 33 mm off, outside the taught range.
2. A lower hover. The wrist camera sees a 3 cm error better from 6–8 cm above the cube than from 15 cm.
3. Find out why the new policy presses on the bin wall at the release. The demos release at the same height as
   before.

Each needs its own pre-registered test.
