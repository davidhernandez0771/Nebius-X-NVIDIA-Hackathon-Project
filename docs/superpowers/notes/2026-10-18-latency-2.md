# Teleop latency 2: live test #7 flicker + recording speed (branch `pm/latency-2`, from `pm/live-7`)

## 2026-10-18 14:40 — The flicker is hand noise, now shown ~1.7× as often because the sim is faster; no sim, relay or send-rate bug. Recording per step cannot get faster without softer camera frames (measured); the ~1 s freeze after Stop is now 0.2 s.

**Bottom line**
1. **Flicker.**
   - With a still fake hand the arm does not move on any branch or render mode (all under a micron).
   - Fast render mode adds no visual flicker.
   - With a still but *noisy* hand (what a webcam sends), the jitter amplitude is the same on every branch. The live-7 sim steps 1.7× faster, though, so the arm shows that noise as ~28 visible jumps per second instead of ~17.
   - The send-rate change (`JITTER_MS`) is not the cause; it changes the jumps by less than 10%.
   - Nothing was changed for it. The fix belongs where the noise is: **T4**, smoothing u, v and yaw in the browser (depth already has a 120 ms EMA).
2. **Recording.**
   - Recorded frames need the cameras rendered after every physics step.
   - Rendering once per control step ("dataset rate", `--render fastest`) is 38 instead of 53 ms per step. Its frames are 15–18% further from a converged render, softer and darker. 2 spp recovers only part of that.
   - The window-side savings (viewport resolution, hidden UI panels) are within run-to-run noise.
   - One real fix: the save after Stop wrote 752 PNGs on one thread, holding the sim loop for 0.99 s. It now takes 0.2 s, with byte-identical files.

### 1. Flicker at the resting position

**Still fake hand** (`still_probe.py`: one held `scripted_hand` pose, paced like `relay_loop`, read every step). Phases: rest (no hand) → still → recording → still. Window in front, high QoS, `--viz kit`.

| Code | Idle step | Tool peak-to-peak | Tool max step-to-step | Joint peak-to-peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `pm/hand-v3` | 55–69 ms | 0.0005 mm | 0.0004 mm | 0.0015 mrad |
| `pm/live-7 --render legacy` | 56–58 ms | 0.0004 mm | 0.0004 mm | 0.0054 mrad |
| `pm/live-7 --render fast` (default) | 33–34 ms | 0.0005 mm | 0.0004 mm | 0.0087 mrad |

The target did not change at all (0.0 mm) in any phase.

**Visual flicker** (the Kit window screen-grabbed around the arm at 14–18 fps from another process):
- Arm still, fast and legacy: 0% of grabs changed (no pixel moved more than 20 levels). No A-B-A frames.
- Under the noisy hand, render noise (mean |frame − 3×3 median|) is 0.091 in fast and 0.093 in legacy. A still arm gives 0.088–0.092. So fast mode adds no speckle.
- The extra changed and A-B-A grabs in fast (39% / 11%, against 17% / 2.5% in legacy) are the noise motion itself, at the faster step rate.
- The `pm/hand-v3` grabs caught other desktop windows on top of Kit, so its visual numbers are not usable. The motion numbers above are.

**Still, noisy hand** (`noise_stream.py`):
- Input: a 30 fps loop with ±1.5 ms frame jitter and i.i.d. per-frame noise. Sigmas: u 0.002, v 0.0027, raw depth 0.02, yaw 0.01 rad. These are illustrative, about 1 px of landmark noise and 2% of palm width; every column scales linearly with them, so the ratios hold for any noise level.
- The depth EMA runs against the last *sent* message, as `handPoseMessage.ts` does.
- Each branch's client throttle (hand-v3: drop if < 33 ms; live-7: drop if < 25 ms) is fed in wall time into each sim. 15 s per run.

| Sim | Client throttle | Steps/s | New targets/s | Tool std x/y/z mm | Tool path mm/s | Jumps > 0.5 mm per s | Yaw path °/s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hand-v3 | hand-v3 | 18.0 | 16.2 | 3.1 / 0.8 / 1.0 | 35 | **16.5** | 8.2 |
| hand-v3 | live-7 | 18.4 | 18.4 | 2.8 / 0.7 / 0.7 | 33 | 17.7 | 9.4 |
| live-7 fast | hand-v3 | 31.5 | 20.2 | 3.2 / 1.0 / 0.9 | 52 | 28.9 | 12.0 |
| live-7 fast | live-7 | 29.9 | 28.5 | 2.8 / 0.7 / 0.6 | 45 | **27.6** | 14.6 |

- **Same amplitude everywhere.** What changed is how often the arm visibly moves: ~17 → ~28 jumps per second.
- In legacy each pose was shown for two renders, at 18 steps/s. Fast shows a new pose in every frame, at 30 steps/s.
- The send rate barely matters. A sim at 18 steps/s can't use more than 18 hands per second. At 30 steps/s, the old throttle's bigger depth-EMA steps give *more* tool motion (52 vs 45 mm/s).
- **The cause is unsmoothed u/v/yaw from MediaPipe**, now displayed faithfully at the faster step rate. That is browser-side (T4). A One Euro filter on u, v and yaw (and depth instead of the EMA) would cut the jitter without the lag of a plain EMA.
- **To confirm on the real input:** a 10 s Record debug trace of a still hand gives the real noise level and its correlation. These sigmas are assumed.

### 2. Recording speed

Fake-hand smoke: 376 recorded steps after a 10 s lead-in, window in front, high QoS. Values are medians, ms per control step.
- Run-to-run noise is ±3 ms: two identical `fast` runs gave 53.1 and 58.3.
- Physics is identical across all modes, so recorded frame k is the same state in every run.

**Where a recorded step goes** (default `fast`, before = after):

| ik target | physics (2 substeps) | render (2 renders) | read | other (per-substep IK bookkeeping) | **step** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0 | 10.2 | 29.9 | 2.6 | 8.4 | **53.1** |

**One render's parts** (`render_bench.py`, ms per `app.update`):

| Part | ms |
|---|---|
| bare `app.update` (viewport off, cameras paused) | 2.4 |
| viewport only (1280×720 Real-Time 2.0) | 8.4 |
| the two 128² cameras only | 9.4 (≈3.5 each, mostly fixed RTX cost; they render `rgb` only) |
| viewport + cameras | 12.3 |

- Isaac Lab cannot render cameras without the viewport: one `app.update` does both.
- Toggling the viewport off and on stalls the next render by 52–93 ms.
- The per-viewport `render_mode` is ignored (the mode is global).

**Options tried, against a converged reference.** The reference is the same physics states with 32 extra renders before each read. "Mean |d|" is in levels; legacy's own run-to-run gap is the scale.

| Mode | Rec step | Rec render | Front mean \|d\| | Wrist mean \|d\| | Wrist bias | Sharpness front / wrist |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| legacy | 54.6 | 30.6 | 0.765 | 1.162 | −0.15 | 0.979 / 1.007 |
| legacy, 11 s lead-in | 51.6 | 27.2 | 0.774 | 1.177 | −0.17 | 0.981 / 1.005 |
| **fast (default)** | 53.1 | 29.9 | 0.783 | 1.189 | −0.14 | 0.977 / 1.005 |
| fastest (cameras once per control step) | **37.4** | 15.0 | 0.892 | 1.376 | −0.27 | 0.972 / 0.989 |
| fastest + `/rtx/rtpt/spp` 2 | 37.8 | 14.0 | 0.881 | 1.294 | −0.09 | 0.966 / 0.987 |
| legacy + spp 2 | 52.0 | 27.3 | 0.781 | 1.128 | −0.02 | 0.971 / 1.002 |
| fast + viewport ½ resolution | 48.8 / 54.8 / ≈ fast | 25.8 / 28.6 | bit-identical to fast | | | |
| fast + Kit UI panels hidden | 52.4 | 29.2 | not checked (display only) | | | |

- **Dataset-rate rendering loses quality:**
  - frames are 15–18% further from converged;
  - they are softer (sharpness −1 to −2%);
  - the wrist camera is darker.
- 2 spp is free and fixes the bias, but not the softness. Real-Time 2.0's quality comes from its per-physics-step temporal history.
- `fastest` stays opt-in. Its cost is now measured: 30% faster recording for visibly softer training frames. Lead's call; given sprint 8's render sensitivity, I would not.
- **Viewport ½ resolution:**
  - the `rec_probe` run gave −4 ms;
  - the real sink trace (render 28.6 vs 28.1) and the render bench (12.1 vs 12.3) gave nothing;
  - so it was noise, and the flag I'd added was reverted.
- **Hidden panels:** −0.7 ms per render, inside the noise; not shipped.

**Non-render parts** (`substep_bench.py`, CUDA-synced, per physics substep):

| Part | ms |
|---|---|
| physics | 4.0 |
| `scene.update` | 1.65 (7 contact sensors are lazy; ~0.3 ms each is the synced kernel launch) |
| IK inputs + compute | 1.6 |
| targets + write | 0.75 |
| read (with frames) | 2.9 |

- These are replay's shared step helpers. Making them cheaper means changing code that replay and sim eval also run, and physics would have to be re-proven identical. Not done.

**Save after Stop** (the sim loop is blocked while it runs; the arm freezes):

| | Save |
|---|---|
| Before: 752 PNGs on one thread | 0.99 s |
| **After: `PNG_WRITERS = 8` threads** (zlib releases the GIL; 16 threads were no faster) | **0.2 s** |

- All 752 files are byte-identical to the sequential writer's.
- `compress_level=1` would also halve the time, but files are 1.6× larger; not used.
